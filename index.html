Countercurrent Multiplier

The thick ascending limb pumps NaCl out, but water can’t follow. The descending limb lets water out, but not salt. Flow runs in opposite directions in the two limbs. Step through the cycle to watch a 200 mOsm “single effect” multiply into a 300 → 1200 mOsm medullary gradient.

Scenarios

Max horizontal gradient the Na⁺-K⁺-2Cl⁻ cotransporter can hold between ascending fluid and interstitium. Loop diuretics drive it toward 0.

What to notice

Small effect, multipliedAt any one level the pump only holds about 200 mOsm between ascending fluid and interstitium. Because flow keeps carrying concentrated fluid down and around the bend, that 200 is stacked level after level.
Opposite permeabilitiesThe descending limb loses water and gets more concentrated on the way down. The ascending limb loses salt without water and gets more dilute on the way up, leaving the TAL at about 100 mOsm.
Urea finishes the jobNaCl alone (switch ADH off) builds roughly 850 at the tip here. Urea recycling under ADH adds the rest of the inner medullary gradient toward about 1200.
ADH decides the urineThe loop builds the gradient. ADH decides whether the collecting duct uses it. Without ADH, dilute TAL fluid passes through unchanged and urine is about 50–100 mOsm.

Teaching model: 8 medullary segments. Each cycle the TAL pumps NaCl until ascending fluid is the single-effect gradient below the interstitium. Descending fluid then loses water until it matches the interstitium; most of that water is carried off by the vasa recta. Fluid then moves one segment. Urea is modeled as a separate inner medullary component that builds with ADH and washes out without it. Numbers are approximate and meant to show the mechanism, not to predict real values.
